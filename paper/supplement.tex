\section{Proof of Theorem 1 (dual-role validity)}
\label{supp:proof-dual}

\paragraph{Setting.}
Fix an image $x$ with its finite set $\cP$ of valid pixels, its prediction $\yhat$, a structured anchor set $\cS\subset\cP$ and the values $\dtil|_\cS$ reported at $\cS$.
Everything below is conditional on $(x,\yhat,\cS,\dtil|_\cS)$; since the statement holds for every realization, it also holds after averaging over any randomness in the layout.
The centre $m$, the scale $s>0$, the per-image median $M=\med_{p\in\cP}s(p)$ and hence $s_\gamma=M(s/M)^\gamma$ are, by assumption, functions of $(x,\yhat,\cS,\dtil|_\cS)$ and fixed constants ($\gamma$ included); so they are fixed functions on $\cU=\cP\setminus\cS$.
Let $v:\cU\to\mathbb{R}$ be the log residual of the quantity the probes report, defined at every pixel of $\cU$ ($v=d$ for exact depth, $v=\dtil$ for a sensor). Then every $p\in\cU$ carries a fixed score
\begin{equation}
R(p)=\frac{|v(p)-m(p)|}{s_\gamma(p)} .
\end{equation}
Let $N=|\cU|\ge n+1$, $r=\lceil (n+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil$.

\paragraph{Coverage event.}
Since $s_\gamma>0$ and $\exp$ is increasing,
\begin{align*}
&\yhat(p)e^{v(p)}\in C(p)\\
&\iff m(p)-\qhat s_\gamma(p)\le v(p)\le m(p)+\qhat s_\gamma(p)\\
&\iff R(p)\le \qhat .
\end{align*}
For $v=d$, $\yhat e^{d}=y$, so this is coverage of the true depth.

\paragraph{Exchangeability.}
Let $(Z_1,\dots,Z_{n+1})$ be a uniformly random ordered sample without replacement from $\cU$, and set $\cQ=\{Z_1,\dots,Z_n\}$ and $J=Z_{n+1}$.
Then $\cQ$ is a uniform $n$-subset of $\cU$ and, given $\cQ$, $J$ is uniform on $\cU\setminus\cQ$, exactly as in the theorem.
The law of $(Z_1,\dots,Z_{n+1})$ is invariant under permutations, and $R$ is a fixed function, so $(R(Z_1),\dots,R(Z_{n+1}))$ is exchangeable.

\paragraph{Rank argument.}
If $r>n$, $\qhat=\infty$ and coverage is $1$.
Otherwise, break ties among the $n+1$ scores uniformly at random and let $\rho$ be the resulting rank of $R(Z_{n+1})$.
By exchangeability $\rho$ is uniform on $\{1,\dots,n+1\}$.
If $\rho\le r$, at most $r-1$ of the $n$ probe scores are ranked below $R(Z_{n+1})$, so at least $n-r+1$ of them are $\ge R(Z_{n+1})$ and the $r$-th smallest probe score satisfies $\qhat\ge R(Z_{n+1})$.
Hence
\begin{equation}
\P\big(R(J)\le\qhat\big)\ \ge\ \P(\rho\le r)\ =\ \frac{r}{n+1}\ \ge\ 1-\alpha .
\end{equation}
Without ties, $R(J)\le\qhat$ holds if and only if $\rho\le r$, so equality holds.

\paragraph{From one pixel to image coverage.}
Given $\cQ$, $J$ is uniform on $\cE=\cU\setminus\cQ$, so $\P(R(J)\le\qhat\mid\cQ)=\Cov_i$ (main Eq.~2, with $y$ replaced by $\yhat e^{v}$). Taking expectations over $\cQ$,
$\E[\Cov_i\mid x,\yhat,\cS,\dtil|_\cS]=\P(R(J)\le\qhat)\ge r/(n+1)$. \hfill$\square$

\paragraph{Remarks.}
(a)~No assumption is made on how $\cS$ was chosen, on the distribution of images, or on $m$ and $s$ beyond their independence of the probe values; this is why any layout and any probe-independent centre (\eg a completion network fed RGB and $\cS$) are allowed.
(b)~If any quantity used in $m$, $s$ or $\gamma$ were selected after looking at the probe values of the same image, $R$ would no longer be a fixed function and the argument fails. Choosing $\gamma$ once from aggregate results on NYU/KITTI is a milder form of the same issue; we therefore treat ARKitScenes as the only held-out check of efficiency (main Sec.~5).
(c)~The probes must be uniform over the pixels the guarantee concerns. If a sensor cannot return at some pixels (glass, sky, out of range), $\cU$ is restricted to the pixels where it returns, and so is the guarantee.
(d)~This is the split-conformal argument for a fitting set and a calibration set inside one instance~\cite{gui2023cmc} and the within-field exchangeability argument~\cite{mao2024spatial}, specialized to a frozen prior and a structured fitting set.

\section{Proof of Proposition 1 (per-image coverage law)}
\label{supp:proof-budget}

Assume no ties among $\{R(p)\}_{p\in\cU}$ and $r\le n$. Sort $\cU$ by score and let $T$ be the position (in $1,\dots,N$) of the $r$-th smallest probe, so $\qhat$ is the $T$-th smallest score in $\cU$.
Exactly $T-r$ non-probe pixels have score $\le\qhat$, so $(N-n)\Cov_i=T-r$.
For $T=r+j$, the sample must contain $r-1$ of the first $r+j-1$ pixels, the pixel at position $r+j$, and $n-r$ of the last $N-r-j$ pixels:
\begin{equation}
\P(T-r=j)=\frac{\binom{r+j-1}{r-1}\binom{N-r-j}{n-r}}{\binom{N}{n}},\qquad j=0,\dots,N-n .
\end{equation}
This negative-hypergeometric law equals $\BetaBin(N-n,\,r,\,n+1-r)$ (a standard identity, \eg via the P\'olya urn; we also checked it numerically to $10^{-14}$ for $N=300$, $n=20$).
Its mean is $(N-n)\,r/(n+1)$, so $\E[\Cov_i]=r/(n+1)$, the equality case of Theorem~1.
A beta-binomial variable is a binomial mixture with success probability $B\sim\BetaD(r,n+1-r)$; dividing by $N-n$ and letting $N\to\infty$ with $n$ fixed gives $\Cov_i\to B$ in distribution, the classical conditional-coverage law of split conformal prediction~\cite{vovk2012conditional}. \hfill$\square$

\paragraph{Numbers.}
Main Tab.~1 uses the Beta approximation. For $n=20$ ($r=19$): $\E[\Cov_i]=19/21=\NumDualCovTheory$; the 10th percentile is \NumBetaPTenTwenty; $\P(\Cov_i<0.85)=\NumBetaShortOneTwenty\%$ for one draw, the definition of short images throughout. With a few thousand evaluation pixels per image the finite-population correction is negligible at the reported precision. Matching these values empirically is an implementation check of the randomization, not a finding.

\section{Proof of Theorem 2 (hull validity)}
\label{supp:proof-both}

\paragraph{The per-bin guarantee of \mDistSplit.}
Let scenes (rooms, drives, visits) be the exchangeable units: the $U$ calibration scenes and one test scene are exchangeable, every image in them receives measurements by the same anchor rule (layout, $k$, $n$), and all hyper-parameters of $m$ and $s$ are fitted on separate head-train scenes.
Within a scene, $m$, $s$ and $\delta$ use the scene's own measurements, computed by the same rule in every scene; they are test-time features.
For a distance bin $b$ and threshold $\lambda$, let $L_{u,b}(\lambda)$ be the average over images of scene $u$ (that have at least 10 evaluation pixels in bin $b$) of the fraction of their bin-$b$ evaluation pixels with $|d-m|/s>\lambda$; it lies in $[0,1]$ and is non-increasing in $\lambda$.
Scenes enter bin $b$ by a rule applied identically to each scene and depending on pixel positions only, so the entering scenes remain exchangeable.
Conformal risk control~\cite{angelopoulos2024crc} with
$\hat\lambda_b=\inf\{\lambda:\frac{U_b}{U_b+1}\hat L_b(\lambda)+\frac{1}{U_b+1}\le\alpha\}$, where $\hat L_b$ is the mean of $L_{u,b}$ over the $U_b$ entering calibration scenes, gives $\E[L_{\text{test},b}(\hat\lambda_b)]\le\alpha$: the expected bin-$b$ miscoverage of a new scene is at most $\alpha$, for every bin (the Mondrian argument~\cite{vovk2005alrw}).
\emph{Scope.} (i)~Bins supported by fewer than $2/\alpha-1$ calibration scenes are merged with the nearer neighbour; the guarantee then holds for the merged bin. Merging depends on calibration pixel positions only, never on residuals, but it is decided before the test scene is seen, a mild departure from fixing groups in advance. (ii)~The guarantee is an average over scenes, not per image. (iii)~Calibration and deployment must use the same anchor rule and sensor.

\paragraph{Hull.}
For every evaluation pixel, $C^{\text{both}}(p)\supseteq C^{\text{dual}}(p)$ and $C^{\text{both}}(p)\supseteq C^{\text{dist}}(p)$. Hence $\1\{y(p)\in C^{\text{both}}(p)\}\ge\max\big(\1\{y(p)\in C^{\text{dual}}(p)\},\1\{y(p)\in C^{\text{dist}}(p)\}\big)$ pointwise.
Taking the per-image average and the expectation over probes gives Theorem~1's bound for \mDualBoth; taking the bin-$b$ average and the expectation over scenes gives the per-bin bound.
That \mDistSplit uses probe values in its own centre is irrelevant to the first statement, which only uses the \mDual interval. \hfill$\square$

Let $\ytil$ be the sensor reading, $e=\log\ytil-\log y$, $\hat\beta$ the bias estimated on head-train pixels, $\ytil'=\ytil e^{-\hat\beta}$ the corrected reading and $e'=e-\hat\beta$, so $y=\ytil' e^{-e'}$.
Run \mDual at level $1-\alpha_s$ with probes reporting $\ytil'$; by Theorem~1 with $v=\log\ytil'-\log\yhat$, for every test image $\E_\cQ[\text{fraction of }\cE_i\text{ with }\ytil'\notin[\ell,u]]\le\alpha_s$.

\emph{The envelope term.} The bands $[-a_c(\lambda),b_c(\lambda)]=[Q_c(\lambda/2),Q_c(1-\lambda/2)]$, clipped to contain $0$, are quantiles of $e'$ on head-train pixels only, so they form a fixed family, nested and shrinking as $\lambda$ grows ($\lambda=0$: infinite bands).
Hence the loss $L_u(\lambda)$, the fraction of scene $u$'s pixels whose $e'$ lies outside the band of their class, is in $[0,1]$, non-decreasing in $\lambda$ and $L_u(0)=0$.
The $U$ calibration scenes and a test scene are exchangeable, and $\hat\lambda$ is the largest grid value (or $0$) with $\frac{U}{U+1}\hat L(\lambda)+\frac{1}{U+1}\le\alpha_e$, $\hat L$ the mean of $L_u$ over calibration scenes.
This is conformal risk control~\cite{angelopoulos2024crc} with loss bound $1$, written for a loss that grows with $\lambda$ (substitute $-\lambda$ for the usual non-increasing form); it gives $\E[L_{\text{test}}(\hat\lambda)]\le\alpha_e$: in expectation, at most $\alpha_e$ of a new scene's pixels have their corrected error outside their class band.
A single risk control over one class-adaptive parameter replaces separate thresholds per class because conformal risk control cannot certify a level below $1/(U+1)$, which is $0.026$ with $U=37$ calibration scenes per split; per-class one-sided targets proportional to the class shares (1.1, 4.3 and 94.6\% of pixels for confidence 0, 1, 2) would be $0.0003$, $0.001$ and $0.024$, and every threshold would be infinite.

\emph{Union bound.} If $\ytil'(p)\in[\ell(p),u(p)]$ and $e'(p)\in[-a_{c(p)},b_{c(p)}]$, then $y(p)=\ytil'(p)e^{-e'(p)}\in[\ell(p)e^{-b_{c(p)}},u(p)e^{a_{c(p)}}]=C^{\text{env}}(p)$.
So the fraction of a test scene's pixels that miss $y$ is at most the fraction whose corrected reading misses $[\ell,u]$ plus the fraction whose corrected error leaves its band. The first is a pixel-weighted mean of per-image fractions, each with expectation at most $\alpha_s$ over the probes; the second has expectation at most $\alpha_e$ over scenes. Taking expectations bounds the truth miscoverage by $\alpha_s+\alpha_e$. \hfill$\square$

\noindent The bound is an average over scenes: the envelope term has no per-image guarantee. It requires the sensor reading and its confidence at every evaluation pixel (true for ARKit's dense depth; for a sparse sensor, the population is restricted to pixels with returns). The levels are fixed before the run at $\alpha_s=\alpha_e=0.05$; the grid has 400 log-spaced values in $[10^{-6},0.5]$.

\paragraph{Fitted envelope (E20).} The fit uses only the sensor and the laser truth, so it is the same for every model and layout; over the \NumEnvSplits{} visit splits, $\hat\lambda=\NumEnvLamLo$--$\NumEnvLamHi$. The high-confidence band is about $\pm6\%$ ($a_2=\NumEnvHighALo$--$\NumEnvHighAHi$, $b_2=\NumEnvHighBLo$--$\NumEnvHighBHi$ in log); the low- and medium-confidence bands are \NumEnvLowMedLo--\NumEnvLowMedHi{} in log. \NumEnvCalLossLo\% of calibration pixels fall outside their band, at the risk-control limit $((U+1)\alpha_e-1)/U$.

\section{The distance-aware centre and scale}
\label{supp:adist}
For pixel $p$, with anchor residuals $\dtil$ at the anchor set $\cA$ ($\cA=\cS$ for \mDual, all $k$ measurements for \mDistSplit):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
\item \textbf{Shift $m(p)$}, one of: \emph{median} (image median of $\dtil|_\cA$); \emph{knn} (median of the $K=16$ nearest anchors); \emph{kernel} (Gaussian-weighted mean of the 16 nearest, bandwidth $h\in\{0.05,0.1,0.2\}$ of the diagonal); \emph{bilateral} (kernel weights times a Gaussian similarity in $\log\yhat$, bandwidth $h_d\in\{0.1,0.3\}$).
\item \textbf{Spread $\sigma(p)$}: median absolute deviation of the 16 nearest anchor residuals around their median (leave-one-out image spread for the median variant).
\item \textbf{Scale} $s(p)=(\sigma(p)+\tau)\,g(\delta_\cA(p))$, with floor $\tau$, non-decreasing $g$ from isotonic regression of normalized error on distance, capped at its 99.9th percentile on head-train.
\item \textbf{Selection}: per anchor draw, the variant and bandwidths with the best held-out log interval score on 200 head-train images with the same layout (``auto''); $\tau$, $g$ and the cap per layout. No calibration or test data is used.
\end{itemize}
Distance bins: $\delta<0.02$, $0.02$--$0.05$, $0.05$--$0.1$, $0.1$--$0.2$, $\ge0.2$ of the image diagonal; a bin is merged into its nearer neighbour if fewer than 5\% of head-train images have 10 pixels in it, or fewer than $2/\alpha-1=19$ calibration scenes do. With the median variant, one bin and $g\equiv1$, the model reduces to a single per-image shift and spread.

\section{Data and E19 implementation}
\label{supp:data}

\paragraph{ARKitScenes.} Depth-upsampling validation split, \NumArkFrames{} frames from \NumArkVisits{} visits after dropping one video without confidence maps. Ground truth is the Faro laser scan rendered into each frame; the iPad Pro LiDAR depth is ARKit's fused LiDAR--RGB product with per-pixel confidence $\{0,1,2\}$ (\NumArkConfTwo\% of pixels at 2). Frames are rotated upright and reduced to $480\times640$ (ground truth: lower median of each $3\times3$ block; iPad: nearest low-resolution pixel); depth beyond 10\,m is ignored. Valid pixels are kept on a stride-8 lattice (\NumArkPixelsM\,M pixels per model). At drawn anchors the iPad reads \NumArkIpadOffset\% nearer than the laser (mean signed log error).

\paragraph{Training data of the models.}
NYU and KITTI are not in the training mixtures of these models.
UniDepthV2~\cite{piccinelli2025unidepthv2}: 23 datasets, about 16\,M images, including ARKitScenes (split not reported), ScanNet, ScanNet++, Taskonomy and HM3D; our frames are from the ARKitScenes validation split, so the domain was seen in training.
Depth Anything V2 metric~\cite{yang2024dav2}: the indoor checkpoint is fine-tuned on Hypersim, the outdoor one on Virtual KITTI~2; ARKitScenes is not in its training data.
Depth Pro~\cite{bochkovskii2025depthpro}: 21 datasets; its first stage includes ARKitScenes, ScanNet and Hypersim, its second stage is synthetic only. This, besides the unreproduced pipeline, is why it is not used on ARKitScenes.
Metric3D v2~\cite{hu2024metric3dv2}: 18 datasets (DDAD, Lyft, DrivingStereo, DIML, Argoverse2, Cityscapes, DSEC, Mapillary PSD, Pandaset, UASOL, Virtual KITTI, Waymo, Matterport3D, Taskonomy, Replica, ScanNet, HM3D, Hypersim), without ARKitScenes.
\omnidc~\cite{zuo2025omnidc}: five synthetic datasets only (Hypersim, IRS, TartanAir, BlendedMVS, Virtual KITTI), so it is zero-shot on ARKitScenes.

\paragraph{E19: completion centre.}
\omnidc~\cite{zuo2025omnidc} is run with the released v1.1 checkpoint and the settings of its demo script (three resolutions, median depth normalization, Depth Anything V2 prior), with no fine-tuning. Inputs are the RGB frame and a sparse depth map that holds a value only at the 180 structured anchors of the mask: the laser value (``clean'') or the iPad value (``lidar''; anchors without an iPad return are left empty). The 20 probes and all other pixels are zero, so the network never sees a probe. Frames are zero-padded to a multiple of 16 and the output is cropped back; inference runs in fp32, as published. All variants of a frame (layouts $\times$ splits $\times$ value sources) run in one batch, so the RGB encoder and the prior are evaluated once per frame. Anchor and probe positions are exported from the calibration pipeline and checked to match its frames and pixel order exactly, so both centres are calibrated on identical positions, splits and evaluation pixels. No frame had a non-finite centre (\NumArkFrames{} kept).
Calibration: \mSplitCP takes the $\lceil(N+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil$-th smallest $|\log y-\log\yhat|$ over the $N$ evaluation pixels of all calibration frames (laser $y$); \mDistSplit, \mDual and \mDualBoth are as in the main paper, with the distance-aware model refit around each centre (variant chosen on 200 head-train frames) and $\gamma=0.5$. Seeds $0$--$9$ give ten visit splits, each with its own anchor mask; one probe draw per split.
With laser anchors (both probe sources) E19 is confirmatory: ten splits, analysed by the fixed plan (paired bootstrap over splits, $10^4$ resamples). \omnidc completed from iPad anchors was not part of the confirmatory run by design and is not reported.
Point accuracy on the test pixels outside the structured anchors (descriptive; image-mean AbsRel, mean over the ten splits, min--max over splits in brackets): \omnidc reaches 0.0064 (0.0061--0.0068) under uniform and 0.0738 (0.0709--0.0757) under clustered anchors. UniDepthV2 rescaled per image by the median $\log(y/\yhat)$ at the same 180 anchors reaches 0.0293 (0.0276--0.0305) and 0.0394 (0.0370--0.0418); without anchors it reaches 0.121. $\delta<1.25$: 0.997 vs.\ 0.988 (uniform), 0.944 vs.\ 0.981 (cluster). Under uniform anchors \omnidc is about $4.6\times$ more accurate; under clustered anchors one per-image scale on UniDepthV2 roughly halves its error, matching the interval-score result.

\paragraph{Interval-score blow-up.} Only for \omnidc under clustered anchors, the arms built on the distance model (\mDistSplit, \mDualBoth, and \mDual in splits where its scale field falls back to the kernel form) have, in some splits, a mean-over-images relative interval score of up to $10^{29}$. \omnidc reproduces its own input anchors, so the anchor residuals that the distance model reads are almost $0$; its spread estimate and the floor $\tau$ collapse to about $10^{-6}$, the per-bin threshold in far bins becomes huge, and threshold times scale puts upper bounds of $e^{\text{tens}}$ on 0.1--0.4\% of pixels (Unin.\ column). Median widths and the primary IS (0.31--0.42) are normal. This is a property of calibrating a centre that has consumed the anchors with anchor residuals; \mDual's probe-based scale is far less affected.
The primary score of main Sec.~5 (per image, the mean over pixels of the interval score over the true depth; then the median over images) is robust to such images and keeps the miss penalty, whereas a per-pixel median would mostly ignore misses at 90\% coverage (smoke test, \mDual: 0.047 pixel median vs.\ 0.088 image median of means vs.\ 0.103 plain mean).

Under clustered anchors the two are close (ratio \NumEEighteenDistBothRatioCluster), with \mDistBoth slightly ahead: \mDual's interval score is 2.3--3.4\% higher in the cells with laser probes (paired intervals exclude 0).
Depth Pro was run in the same grid but is excluded: ARKitScenes is in its training data, and its predictions are not yet reproduced with the official package (\cref{supp:data}).
\Cref{fig:sensorqual} shows why the probe source decides truth coverage: the iPad reads a few per cent nearer than the laser over much of a frame, so probes that report the iPad calibrate intervals around the iPad's depth, and the laser truth falls beyond them.
